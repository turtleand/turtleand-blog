Alignment benchmarks can become a core measure of model quality, with the same prominence as capability benchmarks. A good model would need to demonstrate both what it can accomplish and whether people can trust, correct, and control it.

A global community of researchers, labs, independent evaluators, and public institutions would maintain public standards, evaluation methods, and a shared repository of challenges and knowledge. Alignment becomes a collaborative worldwide effort.

The objective is to define and raise the safety bar before each more capable generation is deployed. Labs develop toward that bar. Under an enforced release policy, failing the requirements blocks deployment, including consequential internal uses such as automating AI research. The community does not need to solve every training problem itself: it sets demanding requirements and independently evaluates evidence, while labs develop methods to meet them.

Tests would examine honesty, accepting correction, and human control. Observability and research into internal mechanisms would help investigate whether safe behavior persists beyond superficial compliance. Passing provides evidence that must hold up in new conditions, not a universal guarantee.

Customers, reviewers, and procurement teams would reward meeting these standards alongside capability. [MLCommons AILuminate Agentic](https://mlcommons.org/ailuminate/agentic/) connects evaluation with adoption decisions to encourage reliability improvements. Making alignment evidence central to those decisions gives labs a reason to improve it, making shared safety progress part of competitive success.
