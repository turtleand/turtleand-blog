A short push on the pedals, and the bicycle carries you farther than your legs could manage alone.

Working with AI can feel like that. A rough idea becomes an outline. A partial explanation becomes a prototype. Even a weak first attempt can gain enough momentum to become something useful.

That leverage is worth using. The question is what happens to the rider along the way.

## Enjoy the Assistance

There is no virtue in making every task unnecessarily difficult. Assistance can help us begin, explore unfamiliar territory, and finish work that would otherwise remain an intention.

But speed does not choose the destination. We still need to steer, notice obstacles, and decide when the route is wrong.

AI adds another complication: its output can look convincing while containing mistakes. The bicycle metaphor helps describe leverage, but it does not remove the need to inspect what the tool produces.

## Keep Some Unassisted Miles

Sometimes it is useful to switch the assistance off.

Write the first paragraph yourself. Sketch the solution before asking for code. Explain the idea from memory before requesting a summary.

This creates a moment in which you can see your own understanding. Where do you hesitate? Which connection is missing? What did the polished answer make seem obvious?

Then bring the assistance back. Compare approaches. Ask about the gap. Use the tool to deepen the part you could not yet do.

The point is to preserve the skills you want to rely on, rather than manually repeat everything a machine can handle.

## Choose the Purpose of the Ride

Some sessions are for getting somewhere. Others are for practice.

On a delivery task, use assistance freely and verify the result. In a learning session, reserve a small part for your own effort before the answer arrives. Confusing those purposes can leave us either needlessly slow or productive without knowing what we learned.

## A Small Experiment

Choose one task this week and spend ten minutes attempting it without AI. Then use AI and compare. Keep a note of one thing you understood and one thing you still need to practice.

The assistance can carry you farther. What do you want to remain able to do when you turn it off?
