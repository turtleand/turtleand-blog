An electric bicycle helps us travel farther with the effort we have. AI can do something similar: turn scattered notes into a plan, or make an unfamiliar task easier to begin. That help is worth having. Three anchors keep us involved in the ride.

- **Choose the direction.** A faster route is useful only if it takes us somewhere worth going. We still choose the destination, question suggestions, and judge the consequences for others. AI can propose a plan; it cannot take responsibility for our decision to follow it.
- **Notice what you understand.** External aids can [reduce mental demands](https://samgilbert.net/pubs/Risko2016TiCS.pdf), but a finished answer is not proof of understanding. Try a small part yourself, explain why it works, and check an assumption. Hesitation gives you a useful place to ask for help.
- **Practice what you want to keep.** Completing work and developing a capability are different purposes. Research distinguishes [immediate performance from lasting learning](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/11/soderstorm_ra_learningvsperformance.pdf). If estimating, writing, or navigating matters to you, make room to use it. Tools can offer feedback and handle routine steps; manual effort alone does not guarantee learning.

**A small experiment:** After using any helpful tool, explain one step without looking. Then check whether your explanation holds up.
