The work keeps coming. A colleague is laid off. A new target arrives. Even after you close the laptop, part of you is still at work, trying to prove you deserve to stay.

That pressure can follow us into studying, caring for someone, or looking for a job. There is something we need to protect, and no clear point at which we have done enough.

I keep coming back to three things.

## Keep a direction that lasts

A job can change before we are ready. A direction can travel with us: explaining clearly, making sound decisions, or helping people through difficult situations.

Choose one quality you want your work to strengthen, then one useful step toward it. You can adjust the route while keeping that direction.

## Separate facts from fears

A colleague's layoff is a fact. "I will be next" is a prediction. Ask what supports it, what remains unknown, and what you can check.

You can prepare for a real risk without assuming the worst outcome is certain. Understanding the situation helps you choose your response.

## Leave room to judge

[Fatigue can impair decisions](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/psychosocial/fatigue.html). Keep room for sleep, meals, and a pause before consequential choices.

Ask what would be enough for today. When a new demand arrives, agree on what can wait. Where possible, give messages and news a stopping point so you have attention left to understand the work and its consequences.

These habits cannot fix an impossible workload. The people setting expectations also need to make them realistic.

**A small experiment:** Choose one unclear demand. Ask what a sufficient result would look like.
